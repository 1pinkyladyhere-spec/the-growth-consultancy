When the Holidays Feel Different: Finding Support During Difficult Seasons

The holidays have a way of bringing our lives into sharper focus. The decorations go up, invitations arrive, and familiar songs begin playing everywhere we go. For someone whose life has changed dramatically, those familiar things can bring back memories of the way life used to be, along with the realization that this year will look very different.

A person going through a divorce may be facing the first holiday without the family traditions they spent years creating. Someone who has lost a relationship, a loved one, or the future they expected may find themselves making plans they never imagined having to make. And while everyone around them talks about what they're looking forward to, they may be trying to figure out how to get through the next few weeks.

There's a particular kind of exhaustion that comes from carrying something difficult while feeling expected to participate in everyone else's celebration. You can love the people around you and still find their excitement hard to share. You can be grateful for what remains in your life and still grieve what has changed. Those experiences can exist together, even when the people around you don't understand.

The pressure doesn't always come from other people. We can place it on ourselves, too. We remember how we used to host the gathering, decorate the house, or make everything special for our families, and we begin measuring this year's experience against a version of life we no longer have. What used to bring us joy can become another reminder of everything we're trying to navigate.

I understand how disorienting it can feel when the life you planned changes and you find yourself responsible for decisions you never expected to make alone. When my own life changed, I had to think about how to rebuild while remaining present for my children. I couldn't simply put my responsibilities aside until I felt ready, and I know how much energy it takes to keep moving while you're still trying to understand what happened.

The calendar doesn't give us extra strength just because a holiday has arrived. It doesn't resolve a difficult relationship or make uncertainty disappear. What it can do is remind us how much we're carrying, especially when we're surrounded by traditions that no longer fit the life we're living.

That's one reason I believe support matters during difficult seasons. There is a difference between having people who care about you and having a private place where you can speak freely about what you're experiencing, without worrying about how your words will affect the people you love. You may need room to talk about decisions you're facing, the life you're trying to create, or the exhaustion of holding everything together while the world expects you to keep going.

I don't believe you have to solve the rest of your life before you can begin taking care of the one you're living today. A difficult holiday season doesn't require you to decide what every future holiday will look like. It doesn't require you to feel cheerful for someone else's comfort, either. What matters is recognizing where you are and giving yourself the opportunity to consider what kind of support would actually help.

Through Starting Point, I work with people who are navigating significant changes and trying to understand what comes next. Healing Without Bitterness is part of that work for people moving through divorce and other painful transitions. My role isn't to tell you what your life should look like. It's to offer a private conversation where we can look at what matters to you and the decisions you're facing, without asking you to pretend this season is easier than it is.

The holidays will pass, but the questions you're carrying may remain. You deserve more than the expectation that you'll simply push through until January. If this season has brought you to a place where you're ready for a different kind of conversation, I'd welcome the opportunity to hear from you.

Elle Lundy
