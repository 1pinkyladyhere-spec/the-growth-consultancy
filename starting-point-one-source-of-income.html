A Place to Begin

I Only Have One Source of Income. What Other Options Are There?

Maybe you've heard people talk about having more than one source of income, but when you think about your own life, you aren't quite sure what another source of income would even look like.

You've always worked a traditional job. You go to work, you do your job and you receive a paycheck. That's what you know.

So when you begin wondering whether there are other possibilities, you may feel as though you're already supposed to know what they are.

You're not.

If you said to me, “I've always just had my paycheck. I don't really understand what my other options even are,” I wouldn't expect you to arrive with an answer.

I'd say, “Then that's where we start.”

You don't need to know what your other options are yet. You can begin by learning that there are other possibilities and paying attention to what interests you enough to want to understand it better.

You don't have to decide that you're going to do anything. Right now, you're just learning.

Learning Doesn't Mean You're Committing to Anything

Sometimes simply looking into something unfamiliar can feel as though you're already considering doing it. But you can be curious without making a commitment.

You can learn about something and decide you're not interested. You can discover that it doesn't fit your life. You can also become interested enough to keep learning without knowing whether you'll ever do anything with what you've discovered.

At this point, you're gathering information and becoming more aware of what exists.

Start With What You Don't Know

If you've spent most of your adult life earning money through traditional employment, it makes sense that employment would be the model you understand best. There's nothing wrong with that, and there's no assumption here that you should stop working in a traditional job.

The question is simply whether you'd like to understand more about what else may exist.

Instead of starting with “What should I do?” you can start with “What am I interested in learning more about?”

Those questions lead to very different conversations. The first asks you to make a decision. The second gives you room to explore before you have enough information to make one.

You Don't Need a Long List of Ideas

If you search online for ways to earn additional money, you'll find plenty of suggestions. But collecting more ideas isn't necessarily the same as understanding your options.

That's why I wouldn't start by handing you a long list.

I'd rather have you begin learning and notice what catches your attention. You may investigate something that sounds interesting and discover that it isn't for you. You may encounter something unfamiliar and want to understand it better.

Both are useful because you're learning more about what you do and don't want to explore.

It's Okay Not to Know Where This Leads

You don't need to know where any of this will lead before you begin. You don't need to decide that you're going to start a business, and you don't need to choose something because you've seen other people doing it.

You also don't need to turn every interest, skill or idea into a way of earning money.

For now, you can learn about possibilities you haven't considered before, ask questions and pay attention to what genuinely interests you. If something does, you can learn more before deciding whether you want to take another step.

You may eventually decide to pursue something you discover, or you may decide not to. Either way, you understand more than you did when you started.

And sometimes that's exactly what you need before you can decide what comes next.
