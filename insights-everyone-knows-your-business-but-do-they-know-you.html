Everyone Knows Your Business. But Do They Know You?

The person behind the company may be what sets it apart.

You’ve worked hard to build a business people recognize. But how well do they know the person who built it?

When you've spent years building a business, it's natural to put most of your attention into making the company successful. You work to establish a reputation, attract the right clients, deliver quality services, and build something people recognize and trust.

Over time, people become familiar with your company. They know what you offer, they recognize your name, and hopefully, they've had experiences that make them comfortable recommending you to others.

But I've noticed something interesting about business owners who have worked hard to establish their companies. Sometimes they've done such a good job building the business that very few people actually know the person behind it.

They know the company, but they don't necessarily know the owner's background, experience, or what led them to build the business in the first place.

And I think that's worth paying attention to.

When people are considering where to spend their money, they often have several businesses to choose from. They can compare services, prices, experience, and customer reviews. In many industries, the differences between companies aren't immediately obvious, especially when everyone seems to be offering something similar.

But there is one thing about your business that no competitor can duplicate, and that's you.

Think about everything you've experienced throughout your career. The challenges you've worked through, the decisions you've made, the relationships you've developed, and the lessons you've learned have all influenced the way you operate your business today.

Those experiences have value, and they may be part of what makes your company different from every other business offering similar services.

Yet many business owners rarely talk about them.

I understand why. For years, the focus has been on promoting the company, not themselves. They've worked hard to build a professional reputation, and they may not see any reason to change something that's already working.

But as your business grows, the opportunities available to you can change as well.

Someone may be interested in working with you because of your particular experience. A potential business partner may recognize that you bring a perspective they haven't encountered before. An organization may be looking for someone with your background to speak, collaborate, or contribute to a project.

Those opportunities aren't always created by a company name or a list of services. Sometimes they begin because someone becomes interested in the person who built the business.

I also think it's important to understand that becoming more visible doesn't mean you need to share your personal life with the world or turn yourself into a social media personality. And it certainly doesn't mean you should be involved in every decision your company makes.

You can build a business that operates independently of you while still allowing people to recognize the experience and perspective you bring to it.

The question is whether the people you want to reach have had an opportunity to see that side of your business.

If you've spent years building a successful company, you've probably developed knowledge and experience that you don't even think about anymore. It's simply become part of how you work and how you make decisions.

But what feels ordinary to you may be exactly what someone else finds valuable.

So as you think about the future of your business, I'd encourage you to consider something beyond your next marketing campaign, new service, or growth opportunity.

How well do people really know the person behind the company?

You've already put years into building a business people recognize. There may be opportunities waiting for you that have less to do with what your company offers and more to do with the person who created it.

And that's something worth exploring.

Elle Lundy
