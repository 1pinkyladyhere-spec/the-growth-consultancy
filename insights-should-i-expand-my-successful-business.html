Should I Expand My Successful Business?

Start With Where You Want It to Take You.

You have an opportunity. A second location. A new market. A partnership. An acquisition. Another revenue stream. The business is successful. The opportunity is legitimate. The numbers may even make sense.

So the obvious question is:

Can the business do it?

I would start somewhere else.

Where do you want the business to take you?

Before you evaluate the opportunity, establish the destination.

For years, you may have been focused on building a successful business. Make it profitable. Create something that works. Grow it. And you did. But where is that success supposed to take you now?

The strategy may have worked exactly as intended. The destination was never clearly established.

Before I evaluate a significant growth opportunity with a business owner, I want to understand that destination.

Tell me about your ideal day, week, month and year. What are you doing? Who are you spending your time with? Where are you? How much are you working? What parts of the business are you still involved in? What do you want your success to make possible?

The more specific that picture becomes, the more useful it becomes strategically.

Then look at what the opportunity actually requires.

Take a second location. The financial opportunity may be excellent. Demand may already exist. The company may have the resources to expand.

But what will that expansion require from you? Will you need to spend the next two years deeply involved in another location? Will you travel more? Will more decisions depend on you? Will it increase your financial freedom? Will it create a company you are excited to lead?

Now we have something meaningful to compare.

Does this opportunity take you closer to the destination you've established or further away?

If the opportunity and the destination conflict, the answer isn't automatically no.

We can explore building it differently.

That could mean examining leadership, structure, systems, staffing, decision-making or the role you play in the company.

The opportunity may still be right. The way you originally imagined pursuing it may not be. That distinction matters.

And the destination is allowed to change.

After understanding what an opportunity could create—and what it will require—you may decide you want it enough to choose a different destination. Then we establish the new destination and build toward it intentionally.

The destination can change. It just shouldn't change by accident.

A successful business gives you options.

The goal isn't to say yes to every opportunity. It isn't to automatically choose the path that produces the most revenue. And it isn't to make the company smaller simply because you want more freedom.

The work is determining what you want your success to make possible, and then building the business accordingly.
