Has My Business Outgrown the Way I’m Running It?

Your business is growing, but running it has become harder.

You have more clients, more opportunities, more people involved or more revenue than you did a few years ago. From the outside, those are signs that things are going well. But inside the business, it may feel very different.

Things that used to be simple take more effort. Decisions keep coming back to you. Your days are full, but important things still aren't getting done. You’ve added help, but somehow you’re still carrying more than you expected.

The business may not have a growth problem.

It may have outgrown the way you’ve been running it.

What Worked Before May Not Work Now

In the beginning, it makes sense for the owner to be involved in almost everything.

You know the clients. You make the decisions. You solve problems as they come up. You create processes when you need them and keep a lot of information in your head because you can.

That can work very well for a long time.

Until it doesn’t.

A business with more clients, more revenue, more people and more opportunities requires something different from the business that got you here.

That doesn’t mean you’ve done something wrong.

It means the business has changed.

The question is whether the way you’re running it has changed with it.

Growth Can Expose What Was Already There

Growth puts pressure on things that were manageable when the business was smaller.

A process that worked with ten clients may become difficult with fifty. A decision you could make quickly yourself can become a bottleneck when several people are waiting for you. Something you could keep track of in your head becomes harder when there are more moving pieces.

At that point, working harder isn’t necessarily the answer.

Neither is automatically hiring another person, buying another system or adding another strategy.

Before adding more to the business, it matters to understand what is actually making the business harder to run.

Your Business May Need Something Different From You Now

The way you built the business and the way you lead it into its next stage may not look the same.

Being highly involved may be part of what made the business successful in the first place. But as the business changes, what it needs from you can change too.

Recognizing that can be the difference between continuing to add more work and creating a business that can support where you want to take it.

What got you here may not take you where you want to go next.

Before You Add More, Make Sure You Know What You Need

When a business becomes harder to run, adding something can feel like the obvious next step.

Hire another person. Buy new software. Add automation. Bring in more marketing. Create another process.

Any one of those could be the right investment.

But if you haven’t identified what’s actually creating the problem, you could spend a significant amount of money solving the wrong one.

That’s why my work begins with a paid Diagnostic Assessment.

Before I recommend that you spend more money, add more people or change the way your business operates, I want to understand what’s actually happening inside the business.

Sometimes knowing what you don’t need can be just as valuable as knowing what you do.

An assessment requires an investment. But so does hiring someone you didn’t need, paying for technology that doesn’t solve the problem, or spending months implementing something that never addressed what was actually holding the business back.

The purpose of the assessment is to help make sure your next investment of time, money and energy is based on what the business actually needs.
