Why Does Everything in My Business Still Depend on Me?

Before you add another solution, understand what is actually keeping you from where you want to go.

You’ve worked hard to build your business. You’ve hired people. You’ve put systems in place. You’ve handed off things you used to do yourself. And yet, an awful lot still comes back to you.

You’re still answering questions you thought someone else would be able to answer. Decisions you thought you had handed off still need your approval. Problems somehow find their way back to you. And there are things that don’t seem to get finished unless you follow up.

At some point, you may start wondering why you bothered hiring help if you’re still involved in so much.

There’s no shortage of advice about what you should do next. Delegate more. Hire a VA. Document your processes. Buy a better CRM. Automate more. Hire another person.

Maybe one of those things would help.

But we don’t know that yet.

If decisions keep coming back to you because your employees don’t have the authority to make them, that’s one problem. If people aren’t clear about what they actually own, that’s another. If the information they need still lives mostly in your head, that’s different again. You may have processes that aren’t being followed. Roles that once made sense may no longer fit the business you have today. Or the business may simply have grown beyond the structure that worked when it was smaller.

From where you’re sitting, all of those situations can feel exactly the same: Everything still depends on me.

But they aren’t necessarily the same problem, and I wouldn’t recommend the same solution for all of them.

Where are you trying to go?

Before I start talking about what needs to change, there’s something else I need to understand.

You may want to open another location, increase profitability or move into another market. You could be thinking about selling the business someday, creating greater financial security for your family or building something your children can eventually inherit.

And what you want next may have nothing to do with making the business bigger. You may want the business to give you room to pursue something else you’ve been dreaming about, more choices about how you spend your time, or simply the opportunity to see how far you can take something you’ve already worked incredibly hard to build.

Those are different destinations. And what your business needs from you now depends, in part, on where you want it to take you.

If you want to open three locations, we need to think about whether the way the business operates today can be repeated without everything running through you. If you eventually want to sell, how dependent the business is on you becomes important in a different way. If you want to pass the company to your children, there are other things we need to think about. And if you want to keep the business intentionally small while making it more profitable, that changes the conversation again.

I want to understand what you’re trying to make possible tomorrow.

Once I know that, we can start looking at whether the business you have today can actually support where you want to go.

The wrong solution can cost you more than money.

And this is where I think business owners can lose a lot of time and money.

When you know something isn’t working, doing something can feel better than doing nothing. So you hire another employee. Buy a CRM. Add automation. Spend more on marketing. Purchase new software. Sign up for another program. Bring in another expert.

Any one of those could be exactly what your business needs.

But what if it isn’t?

You can spend thousands of dollars and months of your time trying to fix something that was never the real problem. And six months later, you may still be dealing with the same issue, except now you’ve added another expense, another system or another layer of complexity to the business.

That’s why I’m careful about telling a business owner what she needs before I understand what is actually happening.

I don’t want to give you a list of things to add to your business because those things worked for somebody else. I want to understand your business. What are you trying to accomplish? What is happening now? Where are things getting stuck? What have you already tried? And what is actually standing between the business you have and the one you’re trying to create?

Because figuring that out has value.

It can keep you from putting money into the wrong solution. It can keep you from hiring for a problem that hiring won’t fix. It can keep you from adding another system when the real issue isn’t the system at all. And it can help you put your time, money and attention toward the things that are actually going to move the business forward.

What got you here may not take you where you want to go next.

The way you’ve been running your business may have worked extremely well. In fact, it may be one of the reasons you’ve gotten this far.

You made the decisions. You figured things out. You carried things because there wasn’t anyone else to carry them. You did what needed to be done, and it worked.

That doesn’t mean everything needs to change. I don’t believe in changing things simply for the sake of change.

It means we need to understand what you’re building toward and whether anything about the way the business operates today is making that destination harder to reach.

What do you want your growth to do for your life?

Because your business is about more than the business.

It affects your family. It affects your financial future. It affects the choices you’re able to make. It may affect what you’re able to leave behind. And it can either help make room for the things you still want to do with your life or make those things harder to reach.

Not everyone wants the same thing, and I don’t think they should.

But whatever your destination is, your business needs to be able to support it.

Start with what actually deserves your attention.

So before we start talking about what you should change, I want to understand where you are now, where you want to go and what may be standing between the two.

If you already knew that last part, you probably wouldn’t be looking for help.

You don’t need another person throwing solutions at you before they understand the problem. You need to know what actually deserves your attention so the next decision you make moves you closer to what you’re trying to create.

If you’ve reached the point where you know something needs to change, but you’re not sure what deserves your attention first, reach out to me.

Tell me where you’re trying to go, what you’re experiencing in the business right now and what you want the business to make possible.

We’ll start there.
