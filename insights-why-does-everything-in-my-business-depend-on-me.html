When Your Business Depends Too Much on You

Your business works. Because you do. What happens when you stop?

A business can be profitable, growing and successful while still depending heavily on the person who built it.

Your business works.

Clients are being served. Money is coming in. You have people helping you. From the outside, it may look like you've built exactly what you set out to build.

But there is one problem.

It works because you do.

You're still the person everyone comes to when something goes wrong. You're making the important decisions. You're answering the questions no one else can answer. You're checking the work, remembering the details and stepping in when something gets missed.

And you may have gotten so used to carrying all of that that it doesn't even feel unusual anymore.

Until you try to step away.

A vacation isn't really a vacation because you're still checking your phone. A day off comes with questions waiting for you. Someone on your team needs an answer only you can give. Something happens with a client and suddenly you're back in it.

The business has grown, but your responsibility for keeping it running has grown right along with it.

That's a very different problem than not having enough business.

You can have more clients, more revenue and more people and still have a company that depends heavily on you. In fact, growth can make that dependence harder to see because everything appears to be working.

Until you're unavailable.

That's when you find out how much of the business still lives with you.

And this isn't necessarily because you have the wrong people or because you haven't worked hard enough to delegate. There are a lot of reasons a successful business can reach this point.

What matters is recognizing it.

Because there is a difference between being valuable to the business and being required for the business to function.

You built the business. Of course you matter to it.

But if you want more freedom, more capacity, continued growth or simply the ability to step away without wondering what will happen while you're gone, it's worth looking at how much the business still requires from you.

Your business works.

The bigger question is whether it can work without you holding all of it together.
