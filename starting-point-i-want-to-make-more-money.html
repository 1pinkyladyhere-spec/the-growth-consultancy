A Place to Begin

I Want to Make More Money, but I Don’t Know What I Could Do

You know you want to make more money.

Maybe you have a job and your paycheck is your only source of income. You’d like to have more coming in, but when you think about how to do that, the obvious answer is another job. Or maybe a traditional job doesn’t work for your life right now. You’re home with your children or have responsibilities that make a conventional work schedule difficult.

You see other people earning money in ways that don’t look like a regular job, and you may find yourself wondering, What could I do?

Before I start answering that question, I would ask you something that might seem unrelated to money.

Tell me about your ideal life. What would that look like?

I want to know what you’re trying to create before we start talking about earning more. Then I would ask you to tell me about your life today. That helps me understand where you are, where you’d like to go and what you hope additional income could support.

If you tell me, “I don’t have any skills that I could make money from. I’ve only ever been an administrative assistant,” I’m probably not going to immediately tell you that you’re wrong. I’m going to say, “Tell me about that.”

I want to understand why you believe it. If traditional employment is the only way you’ve ever earned money, there may simply be possibilities you haven’t had a reason to explore before. That doesn’t mean any particular possibility is right for you. It means there may be more for you to learn about.

You Don’t Need to Start With a Business Idea

When people decide they want to make more money, it’s easy to start searching for answers. You can find endless suggestions about businesses, side hustles, working from home and other ways people earn money.

But a long list of what other people are doing doesn’t necessarily tell you what makes sense for you.

Your own experience may contain things you haven’t considered in terms of earning money. That doesn’t mean every skill, interest or experience can—or should—be turned into income. It simply means you may have more to explore than you initially realized.

Before deciding whether anything deserves serious consideration, you can learn more about it.

Learn Before You Decide

If you come across something you’ve never heard of, you don’t have to assume it’s right for you because someone else is doing it. You also don’t have to dismiss it simply because it’s unfamiliar.

Go look for yourself.

Learn how it works. Read about it. Ask questions. Look at more than one source, especially when the person giving you information stands to benefit from your decision.

The purpose of that research isn’t to prove that something will work for you. It’s to understand it well enough to decide whether you want to keep learning about it.

Sometimes you’ll decide you don’t.

That’s useful too. You learned something you didn’t know before.

Entrepreneurship Is an Option, Not the Assignment

As you explore, you may become interested in starting a business. You may not. I’m not interested in convincing you that everybody should be an entrepreneur.

I’m interested in helping you see beyond the options you currently know so you can better understand what may be possible for your life.

You don’t need twenty ideas, and you don’t need to have a business idea before you begin. You can learn, explore and decide what deserves a closer look without knowing where any of it will eventually lead.

When you started, you may have believed your choices were limited to the ways you already knew how to earn money.

The question I’d want you to consider now is:

What possibilities do you see?
